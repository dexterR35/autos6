# Ten ways to fund the build and create a useful community

These are future experiments, not claims of guaranteed income or features already installed. Start with transparent costs, real progress and delivery capacity.

1. **Sponsor a part with a before/after story.** Let supporters follow one tangible item, then publish its purchase receipt, fitting photos and first-drive outcome. With separate opt-in recognition, credit contributors on that part's completed card.
2. **Supplier match weekends.** Ask a parts supplier to match real contributions to a chosen essential up to a documented limit. Publish the sponsor's terms, actual match and deadline. It makes a small gift's impact concrete without invented urgency.
3. **The Founding Pit Crew.** Introduce an optional paid monthly tier later: early workshop videos, a monthly live Q&A and build-planning polls. Keep the public progress log available, and clearly separate paid membership billing from the free email subscription.
4. **Restoration episode sponsors.** Produce short episodes around a real job—brakes, suspension, paint—and sell a disclosed sponsor slot to a relevant local shop or supplier. Show the actual work and costs so the series builds trust beyond fundraising.
5. **Useful repair guides and checklists.** Sell your own well-tested S6 maintenance logs, sourcing spreadsheets and job checklists. Offer a small free sample. Product revenue diversifies funding and gives visitors a practical reason to return.
6. **Opt-in supporters' wall and car plaque.** Offer consensual credit in the garage and, if feasible, a small physical plaque listing supporters. Publish the recognition rules and delivery costs before promising it; allow anonymous support and later removal.
7. **Community sourcing challenges.** Post a specific hard-to-find part with a clear compatibility checklist and target price. Community members can help without donating. Feature the successful sourcing story and invite voluntary support toward fitting it.
8. **Local garage days.** Organize small, capacity-limited workshop visits or enthusiast meets with a partner shop. Tickets pay for a real event; keep optional restoration contributions separate and publish how the proceeds support the build.
9. **Disclosed affiliate parts library.** Turn the parts catalogue into a useful compatibility and purchasing reference. Use affiliate links only where relevant, disclose commissions and keep alternatives visible. Fund repairs with earned commissions as well as gifts.
10. **The next-build community fund.** After delivering this restoration, invite the community to propose and select the next clearly scoped repair project. Publish who receives funds, how projects are checked and where each contribution goes before accepting money. A repeated record of completed work is more persuasive than endless new promises.

First experiments: one part story with receipts, one real supplier match and a free email sequence showing progress. Measure completion, returning visitors and confirmed contributions; avoid fabricated supporters, countdowns or progress.
